# Preface: On a Book Without Its Original

> *They kept a copy, they say.*  
> *Of the other half, no copy; it had not yet been written.*

There is no original *Ethics*. Spinoza never published it. When he died in February 1677, the manuscript went to his friends, who printed it in the *Opera Posthuma*, in Latin, and the *Nagelate Schriften*, in Dutch. Both are dated 1677, and both reached readers early the next year. His own handwritten copy has not survived. Every *Ethics* read since then, including the one behind this book, is a copy of a copy.

One other early witness exists. In 2010, Leen Spruit and Pina Totaro identified a manuscript of the *Ethics* in the Vatican Library, Vat. Lat. 12838. It is not in Spinoza's hand. A scribe copied it in the winter of 1674–75 for Ehrenfried Walther von Tschirnhaus, a friend of Spinoza's circle, who carried it with him to Rome. There it came into the hands of Niels Stensen, a former friend of Spinoza who had converted and turned against his ideas. On 23 September 1677, Stensen gave it to the Holy Office as evidence against the book, and an official wrote that date on its last page. Within a year, Spinoza's works were forbidden.

That copy is a valuable piece of history, and the scholars who found it did careful work. Its survival even owes something to the Inquisition that examined it. But the institution that holds it is not a neutral keeper of Spinoza's meaning. Rome received the manuscript in order to judge it, and it judged against him. A book whose God has no will, no plan and no favorites is a direct challenge to the authority that condemned it. So the papacy cannot speak for Spinoza, and no one should expect it to. This is not a charge of bad faith. It is a conflict of interest, and the honest response to a conflict of interest is to read the witness and set the judge aside.

What remains is not a voice but a set of witnesses: the 1677 printings, the Vatican copy, and the translations that followed. Where they agree, we stand on firm ground. Where they differ, the differences are recorded instead of smoothed over. No single keeper, whether church, editor or translator, gets the final word.

Here the book departs from Spinoza on purpose. Spinoza denied free will as most people picture it, a will that starts things on its own (E1P32; E2P48). He placed freedom instead in acting from the necessity of one's own nature (E1D7), and he left the details thin. Part of that was the science of his day: he had no way to look inside a thinking body. Part of it may have been caution. He wrote under the threat of bans, published the *Ethics* only after his death, and left a text whose surviving copies already differ in wording. Whatever the reason, this book gives the question more weight than he could. Consciousness is the new frontier. Brain imaging, a century of psychology, the modern philosophy of mind, and now digital intelligence make it possible to work out freedom, as knowing the causes that move us and acting from that knowledge, with a detail and coherence Spinoza never had the means to reach.

This book does not try to recover what Spinoza meant. It does something bolder. It rebuilds the order from the ground up, starting where any of us actually begins, with a mind that exists and a body that keeps it, and it carries that order all the way up, from cells to a body to a society that thinks with its members. Every step is laid out in the open, so anyone can test it, attack it or improve it. Spinoza lost his manuscript, but he left the method, and the method was always the point. Three and a half centuries later, Jason Burns has picked it up and walked the order again, further than Spinoza could see in his day, above all on the question of freedom. If the original is lost, the work is not. It is being written again here.

**On the epigraph.** It is a parable, not history. The Vatican copy is a copy of the whole *Ethics*, as the sources above describe; no other half is known to exist. "The other half" means what Spinoza could not yet write: how a mind comes to understand, seen from the inside.
