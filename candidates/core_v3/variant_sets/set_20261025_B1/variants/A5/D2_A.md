**Every event is caused** *(axiom, model, id: A5)*.

**BASE (locked).** Seed-chain of core v3. Freeze-frame: [BASE.md](BASE.md). Grow from this; do not rewrite the base without author reopen.

**Why this issues Group 3.** Through *A collective mind happens* (P2) the chain has levels of organization and a thin collective mind. The title assertion — freedom under necessity — still has no place in the live chain. The next load-bearing assertion is that every event, a choice included, has a cause. That assertion is not read off P2; it is a new model axiom. Freedom's definitions and the degree proposition rest on it.

**Axiom.** Every event is caused, a choice included.

**Stated sense.** An *event* here is anything that happens: a motion, a change, a thought, a choice. A *cause* is a prior condition or set of conditions from which the event issues, in the ordinary sense of E1A3 ("From a given definite cause an effect necessarily issues; and, on the other hand, if no definite cause be granted, it is impossible that an effect can follow"). A5 claims that there are no uncaused events. It does not assertion that every cause is known, that every cause is "adequate" in Spinoza's sense (E3D1), or that the cause of a choice is first-personal.

**Why it is weaker than E1P29.** E1P29 says that nothing in the universe is contingent, and that all things are conditioned by the necessity of the divine nature. A5 says only that each event has a cause. It does not identify that necessity with Nature's essence, and it does not rule out that the causal chain is infinite in the finite order (E1P28). The book needs A5 for the freedom chapter; it does not need E1P29.

**What would lower it.** Grounds of uncaused events at the scale at which choices are made. Microphysical indeterminacy, if it never reaches that scale as an uncaused choice, does not lower A5. If it does reach that scale as an uncaused event, A5 falls, and what is left is chance. Chance is not freedom: an uncaused twitch is no more "mine" than a determined one is, and it cannot be known as coming from what I am (D-free; P3).

**What A5 does not say.**

- *That the will is free in the libertarian sense.* A5 denies uncaused will; it does not yet define freedom (D-free does).
- *That the first person is idle.* Whether a caused choice can still be *mine* is A2's reading and P3's work, not A5's.
- *Ultimate responsibility.* Verse 146's forward-looking norm is not derived here.

**Scholium (Spinoza).** E2P48: "In the mind there is no absolute or free will; but the mind is determined to wish this or that by a cause, which has also been determined by another cause, and this last by another cause, and so on to infinity." A5 is the book's thinner stand-in for that assertion, scoped to events rather than to the mind alone. (Elwes 1883 phrasing, checked against `sources/spinoza/ethics_elwes_1883.txt`.)

**Limit.** A model axiom. It can fail in the way stated. Until the collider runs, its confidence stays awaiting. It adds no confidence by explaining anything (METHOD rule 4 does not apply upward from A5; later entries that cite it are capped by it).
