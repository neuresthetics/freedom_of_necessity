**What "I" names** *(definition, id: D-I)*. "I", used in a thought, names the thinker of that thought, if there is a thinker distinct from the thinking, and otherwise the thinking itself. *Thinking* here covers what Descartes lists: "a thing that doubts, understands, affirms, denies, is willing, is unwilling, and also imagines and has sensory perceptions" (Second Meditation, AT VII 28; CSM II 19).

**BASE (locked).** Seed-chain of core v3. Freeze-frame: [BASE.md](BASE.md). Grow from this; do not rewrite the base without author reopen.

**Explanation.**

- *Fixed by use.* The word names something only when it is used in a thought. D-I does not say that anything answers to it. That something does, whenever I hesitation that I exist, is what A1 finds. "Thought", "thinker" and "names" are taken as understood.
- *Lichtenberg allowed.* "Otherwise the thinking itself" takes the objection A1 already quotes: "It thinks, we should say, just as we say, it lightnings" (*Sudelbuch* K 76). D-I does not assume a thinker distinct from the thinking. If there is none, "I" names the thinking.
- *Sensory perception in the restricted sense.* Descartes counts perceiving as thinking only as *seeming* to perceive: "I certainly seem to see, to hear, and to be warmed. This cannot be false" (AT VII 29; CSM II 19). In the *Principles* he says that "I am walking, therefore I exist" is sure only if walking means the awareness of walking, not the movement of the body (*Principles* I.9, AT VIIIA 7). D-I issues him here. Only thinking counts, so the word names something whenever it is used, whatever else turns out false.

**What D-I leaves out.**

- *What the I is.* Body, soul or program: D-I does not say. It is a definition of a word, not of an essence. Spinoza counts as belonging to a thing's essence "that, which being given, the thing is necessarily given also, and, which being removed, the thing is necessarily removed also" (E2D2), and D-I claims no such thing. What the author's I is is claimed in *A human body, and its mind* (A2), on grounds.
- *Whether the thinking is felt.* Descartes defines thought through immediate awareness (Second Replies, definition I, AT VII 160; CSM II 113). D-I does not, so it says nothing about feeling. Whether hesitating without awareness is still hesitating is left open.
- *Sameness over time.* D-I names the thinker of *this* thought (or the thinking itself). It does not say that the thinker of one thought is the thinker of the next. A1 says the same ("For as long as I am thinking"). Sameness is not part of this definition. Earlier reasoning that placed it elsewhere is archived with core v2 ([collision_sameness.md](../core_v2/collision_sameness.md)); it is not live in this chain.

**Scholium 1 (Spinoza).** The *Ethics* has no definition of "I". Part Two begins from the human mind: "The first element, which constitutes the actual being of the human mind, is the idea of some particular thing actually existing" (E2P11), and that thing is the body (E2P13). D-I starts thinner. Whether the mind is the idea of the body is not settled here. (Elwes 1883 phrasing, checked against `sources/spinoza/`.)

**Scholium 2 (a digital intelligence).** A system that produces "I exist" uses the word. By D-I, the word names the thinker of that thought, or the thinking itself, *if* producing the statement is a case of hesitating, affirming or the like. D-I does not decide whether it is. D-I takes Descartes's list but not his awareness clause, so whether hesitating without awareness is still hesitating is left open. So D-I fits such a system exactly as far as A1 does, and no further.

**Scholium 3 (Anscombe: does "I" name anything?).** Anscombe argued that "'I' is neither a name nor another kind of expression whose logical role is to make a reference, at all" ("The First Person", 1975, p. 60). If she is right about ordinary use, D-I is a stipulation that gives the book's "I" a reference which the ordinary word may lack: a term of art. A1 survives either way. Its sureness rests on the thinking going on, not on a theory of how "I" refers. If ordinary "I" names nothing, the hesitation still shows that this thinking is going on, and the book's "I" names that thinking by D-I's second clause.

**Limit.** This is a definition. It says what the word names and makes no assertion more likely. It carries no confidence.
