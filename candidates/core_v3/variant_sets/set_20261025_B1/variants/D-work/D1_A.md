**Work, and the seven works** *(definition, id: D-work)*.

**BASE (locked).** Seed-spine of core v3. Snapshot: [BASE.md](BASE.md). Grow from this; do not rewrite the base without author reopen.

**Why this follows A2.** *A human body, and its mind* (A2) speaks of a body's *organization*. To say what that organization does, and later whether a body (or anything else) is a whole, the chain needs a fixed sense of *work*: an activity that helps keep an organization going and is carried out by structures that organization produces or maintains. Definitions 3–6 fix that sense and name seven works. They cite no axiom; they prepare the next claim.

**Definition 3 (work).** A *work* of a thing is an activity that (a) helps keep that thing's *organization* going and (b) is done by structures the organization itself produces or keeps up. *Organization* here means how the thing's components are arranged and act on one another — the structures and constraints they impose — the same sense *A human body, and its mind* (A2) fixes for a body, now said of any thing. A *component* is any piece of the thing, in the plain sense.

"Contributes" is causal: without the activity, the organization would change or fail. No aim is implied. Condition (a) does not ask that the activity be indispensable; it may be one of several ways the organization is kept up. Mossio and colleagues' own example is the heart's pumping: it keeps the blood moving, which keeps the body's organization going, and the body in turn builds and maintains the heart.

Condition (b) is what separates a thing's work from a service done for it. A robot that returns to its charger when its battery runs low takes in energy, and this keeps it running. But people, not the robot, make and repair the components that do it, so by (b) the activity is not a work of the robot's (see *The seven works sort things the way self-production does*, A3, hard cases). An earlier wording of (b), "is itself kept going by that organization", could be read as any loop of cause and effect, and the robot passes that reading. The present wording follows the source (Scholium).

**Definition 4 (performs).** A thing *performs* a work when that activity is going on in it. Whether it is performed is a fact to be found. Whether it is *required* is a separate thesis (PR1) and adds nothing to the finding.

**Definition 5 (as one).** A thing performs a work *as one* when condition (b) holds at the thing's own level: the structures carrying out the activity are produced or maintained by the organization of the thing, not only by each of its components for its own sake, and not only by something outside the thing.

*The take-apart test.* Ask what would happen if the thing's organization were removed while each component was kept going by other means: cells kept alive in culture, people fed and sheltered. If the activity at the thing's scale would stop, the thing was performing it as one. If it would carry on unchanged in each component, the components were performing it separately. The test is a question about what depends on what. It is not a procedure: most of a body's cells die when the body is taken apart, and that death says nothing about which works the body performs as one.

*Example.* People avoid likely sources of infection through disgust. Remove a society's organization while each person is kept going, and each person still does this, so the society is not performing it; its people are, separately. Quarantine and organized public health stop when the institutions that run them are gone, so these are performed by the society as one.

**Definition 6 (the seven works).** The book uses seven works, each fixed by one test question.

| Work | Does the thing, as one, ... |
|---|---|
| **Boundary** | keep an edge that sorts, letting some things across and turning others back according to what they are? |
| **Control** | combine signals from its components, and stored information, into changes that coordinate its components? |
| **Energy** | take in energy or matter from outside and convert it into forms its other works use? |
| **Transport** | move materials between its components? |
| **Signaling** | carry information between its components? |
| **Defense** | detect agents that damage it and resist or remove them, beyond what the boundary keeps out? |
| **Memory** | store information and later use it in its other works? |

The words in the test questions (edge, signal, information, energy, material, agent) are used in their ordinary scientific senses. By this wording control uses signaling and memory: a thing that performs control as one is combining signals and stored information at its own level. *A collective mind happens* (P2) uses this wording for the society claimed in A4; nothing in this definition itself claims society.

**Where the list comes from.** Six of the seven follow the critical subsystems that Miller (1978) finds in living systems at every level, grouped: boundary; decider (control); ingestor, converter and producer (energy); distributor (transport); channel and net, with transducers, decoder and encoder (signaling); memory. Miller counts more subsystems than these (the reproducer and the timer among them), and he has no separate defense subsystem. So the seven are a selection, and defense is the book's addition, drawn from cell-autonomous immunity, immune systems and social immunity (sources in A3; society rows in A4). The selection is the book's own choice and is revisable if A3's test fails.

**Scholium (the organizational account).** Definitions 3–5 follow Mossio, Saborido & Moreno (2009), for whom a trait has a function if and only if "C1. T contributes to the maintenance of the organization O of S; C2. T is produced and maintained under some constraints exerted by O; C3. S is organizationally differentiated." The book keeps C1 and C2 as (a) and (b); "organization", as in Mossio and colleagues, means the structures and constraints that the components impose on one another (Definition 3). C2 says "produced *and* maintained"; Definition 3 says "produces *or* maintains". That is a deliberate departure, so that a structure a thing maintains but did not make, such as a transplanted organ, still counts as carrying out the thing's work. C3, that the system has distinct parts making distinct contributions, is roughly what performing seven distinct works amounts to; the book does not test it separately. Mossio and colleagues use the account to ground the purposive and normative side of function talk; the book uses only the causal loop and takes no position on that further reading.

**Numbering.** Definitions 1 and 2 in this chain are *What "I" names* (D-I) and *What exists* (D-exist). Definitions 3–6 continue that count.

**Limit.** This is a definition. It fixes what the words mean and makes the later tests possible. It does not make any claim more likely.
