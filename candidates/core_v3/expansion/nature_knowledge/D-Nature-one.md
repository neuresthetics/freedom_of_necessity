**Nature taken as one** *(definition, id: D-Nature-one)*.

**Expansion from BASE.** Not a BASE rewrite. Grows from: D-free Nature inline → full def from BASE. Locked spine: [BASE.md](../../BASE.md).

**Statement.** Nature names the one order of causes in which every event sits; strict freedom is of Nature alone.

**Cites.** D-free, A5.

**Scholium (why call it God at all; explanation of a choice, not a proof).** The book does not argue that Nature is God. Calling the one order "God" is a declared reading, carried by the parked full definition ([D-Nature](../../PARKED_VOLUME/D-Nature.md), after Spinoza's "God or Nature," E4 Preface, and E1P15). A reader may decline it and lose nothing the chain derives. Three things follow from treating it this way.

1. *The label is free; what the word usually carries is not.* Declaring the name costs nothing, and for that reason it buys nothing. Reverence, purpose, care, and a reason to love the order are not delivered by the name. Each has to be earned by an entry of its own, or left out. Where the chain does say something nearby, it says it with its own support and no more: awe is wonder plus vastness in the cause-map, and can open reason or freeze into superstition (P-awe); "sacred" describes what a whole treats as inviolable, not proof of anything uncaused (D-sacred-sketch); an event outside Nature's order is denied (P-miracle-denied). None of these rests on the word "God."
2. *Why use the word anyway.* Because it names what the author believes, plainly, rather than hiding it; because it keeps the book in the line it rebuilds, Spinoza's own usage; and because the great traditions, read for what they share, point at one knowable order found in Nature, which this chain argues for without the name. A believer can read the name in. An atheist can read the same entries with the name left out. They part only at the name, not at any derived claim.
3. *The rule this sets.* No entry may lean on "God" for anything "the one order of causes" would not also support. If substituting the one phrase for the other changes what an entry claims, that entry is borrowing from the word, and it is to be rewritten or parked. The same holds for any borrowed word (blessed, sacred): it is a label for what the chain defines, and carries nothing more.
