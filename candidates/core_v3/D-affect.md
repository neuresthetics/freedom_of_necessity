**Affect, and change of power** *(definition, id: D-affect)*.

**BASE (locked).** Seed-spine of core v3. Snapshot: [BASE.md](BASE.md). Grow from this; do not rewrite the base without author reopen.

**Why this follows the works.** Once *Work, and the seven works* (D-work) fixes what keeps an organization going, Spinoza's ground in Part III needs a name for changes in that going — what he calls affects. This definition fixes "affect" in the book's measure (degree of the works), so bondage and blessedness can be stated without a second psychology.

**Definition 17 (affect).** An *affect* is a change in an organization's *power of acting*, together with the ideas of that change. *Power of acting*, in this chain, is the degree to which the organization performs the works that keep it going (*Work, and the seven works*, D-work; *Whole, degree, member, level, self-production, sameness*, D-whole, Definition 8), while those works continue.

**Explanation.**

- *Change of degree.* When the lowest grade among the works rises, power of acting rises; when it falls, power falls. The definition does not invent a new scale beside D-whole's grades.
- *Ideas of the change.* On *A human body, and its mind* (A2)'s declared reading, the first-person side of such a change is not a second thing. Whether and why it is felt stays open (A2's limit).
- *Active and passive (report pattern).* Spinoza: we act when we are an adequate cause; we are passive when we are only a partial cause (E3D1–D2). *Freedom, strict and by degree* (D-free, Definition 15) already uses that pattern for degree-freedom. An affect is *active* to the degree it follows from the organization's own organization and from clear knowledge of causes; otherwise *passive*. This entry does not re-define "adequate."

**Reports (Spinoza, not scored).**

- "By emotion I mean the modifications of the body, whereby the active power of the said body is increased or diminished, aided or constrained, and also the ideas of such modifications" (E3D3). Definition 17 follows that shape in work-language.
- Joy, sorrow, desire: "Pleasure is a man's transition from a less to a greater perfection"; "pain is a man's transition from a greater to a less perfection"; desire is appetite with consciousness of it (E3P11 Scholium; E3P9 Scholium). The book may use those three names in scholia; it does not score a taxonomy of named emotions here.

**What D-affect leaves out.**

- *A full list of emotions.* Not required for bondage and blessedness in this chain.
- *Society's feelings.* *A collective mind happens* (P2) claims activity only. Affect at the society's level, if spoken of, is change in that society's works — not a claim that the society feels.
- *Moral scoring.* Affects are not yet sorted into good and bad; that is later (bondage / blessedness).

**Limit.** This is a definition. It adds no confidence.

**References.**
