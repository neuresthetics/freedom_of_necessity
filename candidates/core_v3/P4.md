**Bondage is acting from what is outside** *(proposition, id: P4)*.

**BASE (locked).** Seed-spine of core v3. Snapshot: [BASE.md](BASE.md). Grow from this; do not rewrite the base without author reopen.

**Why this follows P3.** *Freedom by degrees under necessity* (P3) states the upward degree. Spinoza's Part IV names the downward condition: human bondage to the passions. This proposition states that condition in the book's terms, as the inverse of degree-freedom, still under *Every event is caused* (A5).

**Proposition 4.** A finite agent (the body this thinking goes with, as *A human body, and its mind*, A2, is scored) is in *bondage* to the degree that what it does follows from causes outside its own organization, or from affects (*Affect, and change of power*, D-affect) of which it lacks clear knowledge. Bondage is compatible with every event being caused (A5). It is the inverse, in degree, of freedom in *Freedom, strict and by degree* (D-free, Definition 15).

**Demonstration.**

1. Every event is caused, a choice included (A5). So what the agent does has causes.
2. By D-free, Definition 15, the agent is freer to the degree that (a) what it does follows from its own organization and (b) it knows those causes clearly (P3).
3. Therefore, to the degree that (a) fails — what it does follows from causes that merely push from outside — or (b) fails — the affects at work are not clearly known — the agent is less free in Definition 15's sense.
4. Call that condition *bondage*. Then bondage rises as degree-freedom falls, under the same causes A5 requires, and continuing the works that keep it going (*Work, and the seven works*, D-work) continues whether the works are steered from inside or pushed from outside.
5. So bondage is acting from what is outside, or from unclearly known affects, by degree; and it does not require uncaused passions. ∎

**Cap.** P4 cites A2, A5, and P3. Effective confidence = min(A2, A5, P3) once scored. D-free, D-bondage, D-work, and D-affect are definitions and add nothing. A2's declared reading (first person) adds nothing; the scored half uses A2(a).

**Scholium 1 (Spinoza, reports).** E4 Preface: man is necessarily subject to passions. E4P1–P5 treat how inadequate ideas and external causes bind. E4P66 Note contrasts the slave (led by emotion or opinion) with the free man (led by reason). These reports sketch the same contrast P4 names; they do not raise its confidence.

**Scholium 2 (not a moral insult).** "Bondage" here is a degree of causal and epistemic dependence, not a slur and not a claim that the agent is property. Forward-looking responsibility stays a norm outside this proposition.

**How P4 fails.**

- If P3's distinctive claim fails (knowledge of causes adds nothing that can change how the agent acts), then bondage as the inverse of Definition 15(b) is idle in the same way.
- If freedom requires could-have-done-otherwise, then both P3 and P4 use the wrong definition (libertarian side of the collider).
