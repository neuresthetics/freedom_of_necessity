**Apparent contingency is incomplete knowledge** *(proposition, id: P7)*.

**Why this follows.** *Contingent as incomplete knowledge* (D-contingent), under *Every event is caused* (A5) and *Finite causes form an infinite chain* (A7), yields the working claim about apparent contingency.

**Proposition 7.** If a finite event seems contingent, that appearance is *incomplete knowledge of causes* (*Contingent as incomplete knowledge*, D-contingent), not an uncaused gap in *Every event is caused* (A5). Under *Finite causes form an infinite chain* (A7), the determining finite cause may be unknown without ceasing to be a cause.

**Stated sense.** Contingency is epistemic, not a third ontic status beside caused and uncaused. Libertarian gaps are not smuggled in under the word "maybe."

**How P7 fails.** If A5 fails at the scale of choices, or if contingency is redefined as uncaused possibility, Proposition 7 answers a different question.

**Cap.** Cites A5 and A7. Effective confidence = min(A5, A7) once scored. Definitions add nothing.
