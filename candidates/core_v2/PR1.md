**Principle.** Where the same requirement holds, the same work turns up, in forms that differ completely from level to level.

**Two words.** *Requirement* here is conditional: *if* a thing is to last as one, *then* it must do this work. *Necessity*, the word in the book's title, is modal: what could not be otherwise. PR1 uses only the first. In the core, "must" appears only in this entry.

**The thesis.** Each of the seven works answers a distinct requirement of anything that produces itself (D-whole, Definition 9):

- to stay distinct from what surrounds it, it must sort what crosses its edge (boundary);
- to keep its many works in step with one another, it must coordinate them (control);
- to make up for running down, it must take in energy (energy);
- to supply parts that are not at the edge where materials come in, it must move materials (transport);
- for the state of one part to reach another part at a distance, it must carry information (signaling);
- to survive agents that damage it, it must resist them (defense);
- to rebuild its parts as they wear out, it must keep a record of how they are made (memory).

A membrane and a border look nothing alike. PR1 says why both turn up: the same requirement holds at both levels, and what carries across is the work, not the form.

**What PR1 is for.** It explains why A3's anchors all perform the seven, and it tells a reader where to look at a new level: for each requirement, find what does that work there. The details are found at that level, not assumed.

**What would count against it.** A self-producing thing that lasts while lacking one of the seven works, or one that loses a work and goes on producing itself. Either case would also be an F1 failure of A3.

**Limit.** An explanation adds no confidence (METHOD.md, rule 4). A3's anchors passing shows the works are *performed*; it does not show they are *required*, and PR1 cannot lend A3 or A4 any score. Each row earns its grade from its own evidence.

**What changed.** The earlier PR1 spoke of "any whole that persists". Now that D-whole defines a whole by the seven works, "a whole must perform the seven works" would be true by definition and empty. So the thesis is stated about self-producing things, which D-whole, Definition 9, defines without the seven works.
