**Root.** I exist.

**Why it is certain.** To doubt that I exist is to be doubting. The doubting is going on, so something exists: at the least, the doubting itself. "I" in A1 names no more than that, whatever this thinking is, or is the thinking of. So the doubt proves what it doubts. Descartes reaches the same point in the Second Meditation: "this proposition, I am, I exist, is necessarily true whenever it is put forward by me or conceived in my mind" (AT VII 25; CSM II 16–17).

**Confidence 1.0.** This is the only entry that cannot be revised. The 1.0 is not a probability. It marks the one claim whose denial, made by me, proves it. A1 lends no evidence to anything built on it; it gives the chain its subject. Every later "I" refers to what A1 secures.

**What A1 does not say.**

- *What the I is.* Not a body, not a human, not a lasting self, not a substance. Lichtenberg pressed this against Descartes: "It thinks, we should say, just as we say, it lightnings. To say cogito is already too much if we translate it as I think" (*Sudelbuch* K 76). A1 takes the point. It claims only that this thinking is going on, and so that something exists. What that something is comes at A2, on evidence, and can be revised there.
- *For how long.* The certainty holds while the thinking goes on. Descartes again: "I am, I exist, that is certain. But for how long? For as long as I am thinking" (Second Meditation, AT VII 27, Cottingham's translation). That I existed yesterday is memory, and memory is evidence, not root.
- *For whom.* The certainty is first-personal. My A1 is certain to me. A reader has only evidence that I exist, not certainty; the reader's own A1 is certain to the reader.

**Scholium 1 (an AI).** Because A1 says nothing about what the I is, its wording fits any thinker, and an AI can produce the same sentence about itself. Whether an AI that produces it is doubting or thinking, and so whether the sentence is certain *for it*, A1 does not settle: the certainty belongs to whoever actually doubts, and nothing here decides who does. The book's chain is the author's, and it splits at A2. The author's next step is a human body made of cells. An AI's next step, if it has one, would be a claim about hardware and programs, carried by its own evidence.

**Scholium 2 (Spinoza).** Spinoza's axioms for Part Two start one step later and already include the human: "Man thinks" (E2A2). A1 starts thinner, and "human" moves to A2, where evidence can carry it. (Elwes 1883 wording, checked against `sources/spinoza/`.)
