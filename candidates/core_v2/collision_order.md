# Collision: the order of the page

**Status.** A candidate collision, written 2026-09-29 PT at the author's request ("collide it"). It scores nothing. It moves entries on the page and changes no entry's content, cites or confidence.

**The question.** A1 now uses two defined words, "I" and "exist". Where does it go on the page?

- **Thesis E.** Every definition first, then A1 and the other claims, as in Part One of the *Ethics*. This is the order core v2 used after the second round.
- **Thesis F.** D-I, D-exist, A1, and then the rest.
- A third option is judged too, as asked: the one that emerges from the collision, Thesis T below.

**What the order may not change.** A page order is not a citation graph. No order changes what supports what, or any cap. So the collision is judged only on what an order can do. Does it keep every entry after what it uses? Does it follow the model the book claims? Does it show the reader the structure of the argument?

**Criteria.**

- **K1.** No entry uses a term or cites an entry that comes after it.
- **K2.** Fidelity to the *Ethics*, and to HOW_THIS_BOOK_IS_BUILT.md, section 5: "On the page, definitions come first, as in Spinoza."
- **K3.** The root is met early, and not after material it does not use.
- **K4.** Each entry stands near what it uses.
- **K5.** One simple rule, stable when entries are added.
- **K6.** The order does not suggest a dependence that is not there.

Spinoza's structure was checked in `sources/spinoza/ethics_elwes_1883.txt`.

---

## Thesis E: all definitions first, then A1

**E1 (the model).** Part One of the *Ethics* opens with eight definitions, then seven axioms, then the propositions. HOW, section 5, says: "On the page, definitions come first, as in Spinoza." Order E does exactly that.

**E2 (neutral by kind).** Definitions carry no confidence and support nothing. Putting all of them first puts nothing weighty ahead of A1. It only fixes the vocabulary before any claim is made.

**E3 (the simplest check).** With every definition first, K1 is checked at a glance: no claim can use a word defined after it, because none is. The rule, "definitions, then claims", needs no judgment and does not change when entries are added.

**E4 (the reader knows where to look).** A reader who wants the meaning of a word looks in one place.

**Conclusion of E.** Keep the order: D-I, D-exist, D-work, D-whole, D-Nature, then A1, A2, A3, PR1, A4, P1, P2.

---

## Thesis F: D-I, D-exist, A1, then the rest

**F1 (the root comes before what it does not use).** A1 uses only D-I and D-exist. Under E, the reader meets the seven works, the grades of wholeness and the definition of Nature before the root, and none of these has anything to do with the root. That misleads (K6). The core was rebuilt "from I exist", and the page should show it.

**F2 (dependence order).** A proof is easiest to check when each step follows what it uses. A1 then stands third, not sixth.

**F3 (Spinoza does not put every definition first).** The *Ethics* as a whole does not follow E.

- Each of Parts One to Four sets out its own definitions at its head, after a preface in Parts Three and Four. The definitions of Part Three are not given in Part One.
- Part Two stops after E2P13 for a run of axioms, lemmas, a definition (of an individual), and postulates. That definition is placed where it is used, in the middle of the Part.
- Part Three gathers the definitions of the emotions at its end.
- Part Five opens with axioms, not definitions.

So "all definitions first" copies Part One, not the *Ethics*.

**Conclusion of F.** D-I, D-exist, A1, then D-work, D-whole, D-Nature, A2, A3, PR1, A4, P1, P2.

---

## Thesis T (the option that emerges): groups, each opening with its definitions

F3 shows what Spinoza actually does: definitions first *within each Part*. F2 shows why: each claim near what it uses. Applied to core v2, this gives three groups. The definitions come first in each, and the claims follow.

1. **The I.** D-I, D-exist; A1, A2. A2 uses only D-I, and cites only A1.
2. **Wholes.** D-work, D-whole, D-Nature; A3, PR1. These use the definitions of groups 1 and 2 only.
3. **Society and levels.** A4, P1, P2. These use definitions from groups 1 and 2, and cite A2, A3 and A4.

Definitions keep their numbers (1–2, then 3–13), which still rise down the page.

## Side by side

| Criterion | E (all definitions first) | F (D-I, D-exist, A1, rest) | T (groups) |
|---|---|---|---|
| K1 no forward use | passes | passes | passes |
| K2 the *Ethics*, and HOW §5 | follows Part One, and HOW's words literally | follows neither: definitions after the first claim, with no rule for which | follows the *Ethics* as a whole: definitions head each group, as they head Parts One to Four. HOW's "definitions come first" holds within each group. |
| K3 root met early | A1 sixth, after works, wholes and Nature | A1 third | A1 third |
| K4 each entry near what it uses | A2 seventh, while its only term is first | A2 seventh, after three definitions it does not use | A2 fourth, next to A1, whose I it identifies |
| K5 simple, stable rule | simplest: "definitions, then claims" | no stated rule; adding a definition raises the question again | "each group opens with the definitions its claims use": one judgment per new entry (which group) |
| K6 no false suggestion | can suggest that the root rests on biology and on Nature | suggests A2 rests on D-work, D-whole and D-Nature | suggests nothing false: the I, then wholes, then society |

**E's strongest points, answered.**

- **E1.** It follows Part One of the *Ethics*, not the *Ethics*. HOW's sentence says "as in Spinoza", and Spinoza's practice is per Part. T keeps the letter of HOW within each group.
- **E2.** True, and it does not decide anything: under every option, definitions carry no confidence.
- **E3.** E's check is simpler, but T's check is almost as simple (K1 passes by the same scan), and E buys that simplicity with K3, K4 and K6.
- **E4.** Under T, a reader looking for a word looks at the head of one of three groups. The README lists every definition with its number.

**F's strongest point, answered.** F is right about A1, and T keeps it. But F has no rule for the rest, and it leaves A2, the entry that says what A1's I is, behind three definitions it does not use. T is F's own reason, dependence order, applied to every entry.

## Verdict

**T.** Order E falls on K3, K4 and K6. Order F keeps A1 early but has no rule, and it separates A1 from A2. Order T gives three groups, each opening with its definitions, as each of Parts One to Four of the *Ethics* sets out its own at its head:

- **The I:** D-I, D-exist, A1, A2
- **Wholes:** D-work, D-whole, D-Nature, A3, PR1
- **Society and levels:** A4, P1, P2

A1 is the first claim and the third entry. A2 follows it, so the page reads: what "I" and "exist" mean, that I exist, what I am. Only then come the tools for wholes.

**What survives from E.** HOW's "definitions come first" is kept within each group. HOW would need one word changed on promotion: "On the page, definitions come first *in each group*, as in each Part of the *Ethics*." See README, section 5a.

**What survives from F.** A1 immediately after its two definitions.

**What stays open.** Nothing needs the author, except whether to call the groups "Parts". The collision does not decide that. Nothing in core v2 is long enough to need the name.
