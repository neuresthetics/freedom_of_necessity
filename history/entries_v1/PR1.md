**Principle.** Structure follows function, and function follows requirement. The same requirement holds at every scale; the expression changes completely from scale to scale, but the pattern does not.

**Two words.** *Requirement* here means what a whole must do if it is to last. It is conditional: if a thing is to persist as a whole, then it must do this work. *Necessity*, in the book's title, is modal: what could not be otherwise. PR1 uses the first sense only.

**Explanation.** Any whole that persists must do certain work: hold a boundary, coordinate its parts, take in energy, move materials, pass signals, defend itself, and remember. This is PR1's thesis: these functions are required by the fact of being a whole, not by the material the whole is made of. A membrane and a border look nothing alike, yet each is what a whole needs in order to stay a whole. What carries across levels is the function, not the form.

**Method.** Correspondences between levels (see the A3 table) are judged on whether the same function is performed at each level, not on surface resemblance. PR1 predicts that it will be; the evidence has to show that it is. Knowing the pattern shows where to look at a new scale; the details of expression are found there, not assumed.

**Limit.** This principle explains why the rows should line up. It does not raise their confidence. Each row still earns its score from its own evidence, and A3 remains capped by its weakest row.
