# Axioms of Necessity (v12, draft)

*A verse companion to* Freedom of Necessity

> **v12.** Derived fresh from core v2, the core in force since 2026-10-15 (`entries/`). The verses follow the core's order: the I (D-I, D-exist, A1, A2), wholes (D-work, D-whole, D-Nature, A3, PR1), society and levels (A4, P1, P2), then the first person, freedom, God or Nature, and the close. One chain, no split tracks. Verses that still fit were kept from v11; what changed and why is in `v12_changes.md`. Numbering is new and does not match v11.

> Each verse ends with a small tag naming the entry it rests on. *scholium* marks a remark on that entry (a report, a comparison, an image) that proves and scores nothing. *reading* marks a declared reading, which is stated and not scored. *thesis* marks the free-will thesis, which the core does not yet contain and the book assumes; *Italic lines* state it. † marks a norm the book doesn't derive; ‡ marks a claim still waiting for its entry or its evidence. No verse claims more than its entry. No scores are valid until the steel-man collider runs.


# Part One: The Verses

## I. The Start

*Begin where anyone begins: with a thought going on.*

1. I exist. [A1]

2. To doubt it is to be doubting, and the doubting is going on. The doubt proves what it doubts. [A1]

3. "I" names the thinker of this thought, if there is one apart from the thinking. If there is none, it names the thinking itself. [D-I]

4. It thinks, Lichtenberg said, as we say it lightnings. The start allows it: "I" need name no more than the thinking. [D-I]

5. An activity exists while it is going on. A thing lasts while it goes on existing. [D-exist]

6. So the start is thin. This thinking is going on, now. It says nothing of what I am, or for how long. [A1]

7. That I existed yesterday is memory, and memory is evidence, not root. [A1]

8. The certainty is mine alone. You have evidence that I exist; your own start is certain only to you. [A1]

9. Only the start is beyond revision: I exist. Everything else stands on something, and stands no higher than what holds it up. [A1]

10. Thinking, here, is doubting, understanding, affirming, denying, willing, refusing, imagining, and seeming to perceive. It is not defined by being felt. [D-I]

11. So the word fits any thinker, a machine included. Whether a machine that says "I exist" is doubting, the start does not settle. [D-I, A1]

12. The floating man, touching nothing, still knows that he exists. From this, Ibn Sina concluded that the self is a substance apart from the body; I don't follow him there. [A1, scholium]


## II. The Body

*What the I is comes next, and it comes on evidence.*

13. I am a human body made of cells, and of what cells make: bone, the matrix of skin and tendon, the fluids between. [A2]

14. "Human" is not given by the start. My anatomy and my descent earn it, as they would for anyone. [A2]

15. I contain multitudes: some thirty trillion of my own cells, most of them red blood cells, and roughly as many bacteria. [A2, D-whole]

16. Close these eyes and my seeing ends. The thinking goes with this body. [A2]

17. Damage one region and speech goes. Remove another and new memories no longer hold. Where a structure fails, an ability goes with it. [A2]

18. So much is evidence: I am made of this body, and this thinking goes with it and varies with it. [A2]

19. That I am nothing other than this body, the evidence does not decide. I read it so. That is a declared reading, not a finding. [A2, reading]

20. My mind is this body's organization described in the first person: one thing, told two ways. This too is a reading. The evidence fits it, and fits its rival, that the body produces a second thing. [A2, reading]

21. I take the reading with one thing in it rather than two. That is a reason of economy, not evidence. [A2, reading]

22. The order and connection of ideas is the same as the order and connection of things. [A2, scholium]

23. The mind is the idea of the body; whatever happens in the body, the mind perceives in its own way. [A2, scholium]

24. No one has yet determined what the body can do. [A2, scholium]

25. Why any of it is felt, why there is something it is like to be this body, stays undecided. "Described in the first person" says where feeling belongs, not why there is any. [A2]

26. Spinoza held that all things are animated, in different degrees. I claim less: one body, mine, and its first-person description. [A2, scholium]


## III. Work

*What a thing does to keep itself going.*

27. A work is what a thing does that keeps its own organization going, done by structures that organization makes or maintains. [D-work]

28. The heart pumps; the pumping keeps the body going; the body builds and keeps the heart. A work is a loop, not an aim. [D-work]

29. What looks like purpose in a living thing is often a work: a loop that keeps it going, aimed at nothing. [D-work, scholium]

30. A service done for you is not your work. A robot that docks to recharge is kept by the people who make and mend its components. [D-work]

31. A thing performs a work when the work is going on in it. Whether it must is another question. [D-work]

32. There are seven works: keep an edge that sorts, steer, feed, carry, signal, defend, remember. [D-work]

33. A membrane doesn't decide what enters. It sorts, by what it is made of. [D-work]

34. To steer is to combine signals and memory into one coordinated change. Steering already uses the other two. [D-work]

35. A work is done as one when the structures doing it belong to the thing's own level, not to each component for itself, and not to something outside. [D-work]

36. Take a society apart, keep each person fed, and each still shrinks from what disgusts them. That avoidance was theirs. What stops is the quarantine. [D-work]

37. The seven are a selection, drawn mostly from one map of living systems, with defense added. If the test fails, the list changes. [D-work]


## IV. The Whole

*Inside Nature, some things hold together, and by degrees.*

38. Grade each work: zero if it isn't done as one, one if it is done in part, two if it is done steadily throughout. [D-whole]

39. A thing is as whole as its lowest grade. A hurricane's energy doesn't give it a memory. [D-whole]

40. Above zero there is no line to cross. Zero is not a line drawn; it is a work missing. [D-whole]

41. How whole a thing is, and how sure we are of it, are two questions. Never answer one with the other. [method]

42. A member is something whose lasting depends on a whole's works, and which takes part in at least one of them: a shared fate, and a function. [D-whole]

43. Strangers can become members. The mitochondrion descends from a free-living bacterium. Membership is judged by fate and function, not by origin. [D-whole]

44. The bacteria in my gut are not my cells. They feed on me and help train my defenses, so they are members of me. [D-whole]

45. Membership is had for a time. A visitor who depends on a society's works and takes part in them is a member while staying. [D-whole]

46. A hand cut from the body is a hand in name only. [D-whole, scholium]

47. One whole is a level up from another when the second is its member. Levels are counted from a member, not rungs on one ladder. [D-whole]

48. A thing produces itself when its own activities make and replace the components that carry them out, the components of its edge included. [D-whole]

49. A whole is the same whole later while its organization has lasted, with no full stop and no branching. [D-whole]

50. A body keeps its nature while its parts are replaced, so long as the new parts keep the same proportion of motion and rest among themselves. [D-whole, scholium]

51. Most of my cells, by count, are red blood cells, and each lasts about four months. The shape stays; the matter flows. [D-whole, A3]

52. A worm in the blood would take each drop for a world and never see the body. We live in our corner of nature as that worm lives in the blood. What it knows is true of what it touches; it turns false only when taken for a picture of the whole. [D-whole, scholium]


## V. Nature

*Everything, with nothing besides it.*

53. There is one order, and it has no outside. Whatever is, is in it. Call it God, or Nature. [D-Nature]

54. Nature is everything that exists, taken together, with nothing besides it: taken as one, not shown to be one. [D-Nature]

55. Nothing besides it can limit it, so it is not finite, and it is not graded. It is neither a whole nor a non-whole. [D-Nature]

56. Everything is in Nature, and so "in Nature" sorts nothing. My cells are members of me, and I am a member of my society; none of us is a member of Nature. [D-Nature]

57. I can't fail to be in Nature. [D-Nature]

58. That Nature is truly one, one substance, and that it is God, is Spinoza's reading and mine. It is declared, not shown, and it carries no score. [D-Nature, reading]

59. Self-caused and self-producing share a word, not a concept. [D-Nature]


## VI. The Test

*Does the measure find anything?*

60. The seven works sort things the way self-production does. [A3]

61. Whatever makes and replaces its own components does all seven as one. Whatever lasts without doing so fails at least one. [A3]

62. Self-production is a second test, defined without the seven, so that their agreement is a finding and can fail. [A3]

63. A free cell, a body cell, a body: each makes itself and should pass. That they do is expected, and weak evidence. [A3]

64. A stone lasts, a hurricane feeds, an archive remembers. Each should fall short of some work. If one passed all seven, the seven would be too loose to find anything. [A3]

65. A candle flame feeds itself on its own heat, but it uses up its components instead of making them. [A3]

66. A virus outside a cell takes nothing in. Inside one, the host does its work for it. [A3]

67. A red blood cell has given up its nucleus. It can't remake itself and lasts about four months: a member of me, not a whole. [A3, D-whole]

68. The robot that docks is the hardest case. It passes only if the repairs others make count as its own, and they don't. [A3]

69. By the same test, today's AI systems are not wholes, since people build and keep their hardware. That says nothing about whether they think, or whether anything is felt. [A3]

70. A claim that nothing could lower isn't a claim. The start is the one exception. [A3, method]


## VII. The Pattern

*Same requirement, completely different clothes.*

71. Where the same requirement holds, the same work turns up, in forms that differ completely from level to level. [PR1]

72. Structure follows function, and function follows requirement. The expression changes completely at each scale; the pattern does not. [PR1]

73. Requirement is conditional: if a thing that makes itself is to go on making itself, it must do this work. Necessity is what could not be otherwise. The title speaks of the second; this chapter, of the first. [PR1]

74. To make up for running down, it must feed. To keep its works in step, it must steer. To stay itself while things cross, its edge must sort. [PR1]

75. Where materials lie farther than diffusion reaches, it must carry. Where one component's state must reach another far off, it must signal. Where harm is present, it must defend. [PR1]

76. Whatever rebuilds itself must keep what it needs to rebuild, and use it again. That much memory is required; a separate record is a finding, not a must. [PR1]

77. Likeness proves nothing, and a requirement only says where to look. What carries across is the work, found done at each level. [PR1]

78. An explanation is a lamp, not a proof. It shows where to look, and it tests what is offered as evidence, though never the evidence for itself; only evidence that holds up settles what you find. [PR1, method]

79. A city is not a body. As a body grows, its pace of life slows; as a city grows, its wages and inventions speed up. [PR1, A4]


## VIII. The Society

*The whole I am a member of.*

80. The society I live in is a whole to a degree, judged one work at a time, and I am one of its members. [A4]

81. By a society I mean a population under one government, with all its institutions: courts and markets, families and schools. The state is one institution among them. [A4]

82. Its edge is borders, customs and rules of membership. Disperse the posts and no one sorts on its behalf; each of us can still lock a door. [A4]

83. It steers through government, law, courts and markets. Prices carry knowledge no one holds whole. [A4]

84. It feeds through farms, fuels and grids. That most of us could not feed ourselves alone speaks to membership, not to wholeness. [A4]

85. It carries on roads, rails, pipes and supply chains. It signals in speech and writing, wires, broadcasts and prices. [A4]

86. Bodies defend themselves, and so do societies: members by disgust and avoidance, the society by public health and quarantine. A crowd that shrinks from the sick is not yet a quarantine. [A4, D-work]

87. It remembers in records, archives, law and schools. Memory, Halbwachs argued, is carried by a society's frameworks, not by each mind alone. [A4]

88. Each work stands on its own evidence, and the claim stands no higher than its weakest. [A4]

89. "To a degree" means at least one on every work, and two on none claimed. It is not a claim that a society is an organism. [A4]

90. The claim is about the society I live in. It says nothing yet about bands, empires, or humankind. [A4]

91. The picture is old: the belly and the limbs; the eye that can't say to the hand, "I have no need of you"; the commonwealth drawn as a giant made of citizens; the body seen as a state of cells. [A4, scholium]

92. Nothing is more useful to a human than another human. [A4, scholium]


## IX. Levels

*I stand at the seam.*

93. I am a whole at one level and a part at the next. [P1]

94. My body makes itself, so it does all seven works as one. Its cells depend on it and take part in its works. It is a whole whose members are cells. [P1]

95. My society does the seven, each at least in part, and I am its member. It is a whole one level up. [P1]

96. The member and the whole are one body, the one this thinking goes with, lasting without a full stop. [P1]

97. I am many things bound into one, a whole to my cells and a member of my society. I stand at the seam. [P1]

98. On the scored reading, it is the body I am made of that stands there. On my declared reading, it is I. [P1, reading]

99. My groups stand between me and my society, and they too are a level up from me. I act on my society mostly through them, somewhat as a cell acts on the body through its organs. [P1]

100. My cells can't leave me. I can leave a society, and my interests can quarrel with it. The quarrel lowers the society's grade, not mine. [P1, D-whole]

101. The levels stop short of Nature, which is not a whole and has no members. [P1, D-Nature]


## X. The Collective Mind

*An activity, not a thing.*

102. A collective mind happens in the society I live in. [P2]

103. By that name I mean steering done as one, with the signals and the memory steering uses. Nothing more is meant. [P2]

104. Through courts, offices, markets and records, signals and stored knowledge become coordinated action, as one process. [P2]

105. A society's mind is not a thing it owns. It happens, the way a whirlpool happens in a river. It exists as an activity exists: while it goes on. [P2]

106. It happens to the degree of the weakest of three works: steering, signaling, remembering. [P2]

107. That it feels is not claimed. [P2]

108. The community has no corporate consciousness, Spencer said. Spinoza's view would allow more. Undecided. [P2, scholium]

109. The word "mind" marks a likeness of works: my nervous system, too, combines signals and memory into action. It doesn't carry the first person across. [P2]

110. Napoleon, Tolstoy thought, didn't steer history; he was carried by the movements of millions. [P2, scholium]

111. A society can remember what no one alive lived through. [P2, A4]


## XI. The First Person

*Where this book means to go further than Spinoza could.*

112. Every thought I have is had from here. A vantage is a location, not a flaw. [A1, D-Nature]

113. *No third-person description delivers what it is like to be a bat. No first-person feeling delivers the causal chain. Neither outranks the other.* [A2, thesis]

114. On my reading, the first person is no ghost beside the body and no illusion laid over it. It is the body's organization, as it is for the one whose body it is. [A2, reading]

115. Why it is felt at all stays undecided. Undecided is a state, not a failure. [A2]

116. "I" is where the many are counted as one. Most of what I do is never counted. [D-I, A2, reading]

117. Looking inward, Hume found only a bundle of perceptions; the Buddha taught that no lasting self is found among a person's parts. I claim nothing lasting, and the start allows a thinking with no thinker. I part from them only if "no self" means "no I at all." [D-I, A1]

118. If a machine's "I exist" is ever certain for it, what it is made of won't be cells. Whether anything is felt there, nothing here decides. [A1, A2]

119. To dismiss a machine's opinion as merely subjective, meaning that it comes from a subject rather than that it is biased, is already to speak as if it had a place to stand. That grants a point of view at work, not one that is felt; whether anything is felt there stays undecided. [D-I, reading]

120. Spinoza had no way to look inside a thinking body. We have begun to, and have begun to build minds. Consciousness is the frontier, and freedom is the question it carries. [A2, scholium]


## XII. Knowing and Feeling

*Knowing changes feeling.*

121. There are three kinds of knowing: opinion, from hearsay and loose experience; reason, working from notions common to all things; and intuition, which sees a thing from its essence. [scholium]

122. An affect is a change in the body's power of acting, together with the idea of that change. [A2, scholium]

123. Each thing, as far as it is in itself, strives to persist in its being, and that striving is its very essence, not a goal it holds in view. [D-work, scholium]

124. We don't desire a thing because we judge it good. We call it good because we desire it. [scholium]

125. Reason doesn't overrule a passion. It works by becoming a stronger affect. [scholium]

126. A passion stops being a passion once we form a clear and distinct idea of it, and the mind has more power over the affects the more it understands things as necessary. [scholium]


## XIII. Freedom

*One act, two true descriptions.*

127. ‡ Every event is caused, a choice included. No entry carries this yet; it is assumed here, and it can be lowered. [candidate]

128. *Every choice is one event with two descriptions. In the third person, it is a cause in the chain. In the first person, it is mine. Neither outranks the other.* [A2, thesis]

129. *The first-person description isn't idle. For many questions it is the better cause: it names the level that made the difference.* [thesis]

130. People think they are free because they know their actions and not the causes. A stone set moving, if it could think, would believe it moved by its own wish. [scholium]

131. *What dies is the uncaused will. What lives is the first person: still mine, only not uncaused.* [thesis]

132. Freedom is not an escape from necessity. Strictly, only what exists and acts from its own nature alone is free, and only God, or Nature, is like that. [D-Nature, scholium]

133. Every whole takes its energy from something else. No whole that can be graded is free without degree. Finite things are free by degrees. [D-Nature, D-whole]

134. *We act when we are the adequate cause of what happens, and we are passive when we are only a partial cause. The more of what you do follows from your own nature, the freer you are.* [thesis]

135. *Freedom doesn't need that you could have done otherwise, given all that came before. It needs that what you did came from you.* [thesis]

136. *To understand a cause is to know where it came from: genes, schooling, or a meddler's hand. A cause seen at its source can change: sometimes it loosens, sometimes it steadies into resolve. And sometimes you see it whole and are still bound, because it is necessary. Freedom is not escaping the cause. It is knowing it, origin and all, and acting from that knowledge.* [thesis]

137. Freedom that is only an inner cause at work, Kant said, is the freedom of a turnspit, which, once wound up, turns by itself. *But a turnspit doesn't understand what moves it, and here these verses part from Kant.* [thesis]

138. *No finite thing is its own cause, so no one is ultimately responsible. Responsibility can still look forward: to repair, to restraint, to learning.* [thesis]

139. A person acts freely, it is said, when moved by a desire they want to be moved by. What speaks for the person is not a higher desire but a clearer understanding. [thesis]

140. *Freedom grows with insight into necessity. To understand a passion clearly is to begin to make it an action, and so to become more fully its cause.* [thesis]

141. Understanding a cause moves us less by it and more from it. [thesis]

142. Cook Ding's knife stayed sharp for nineteen years because it followed the grain of the ox. Skill is freedom found inside necessity. [thesis, scholium]

143. ‡ A society may be freer as more of its members act from their own nature, and how many can is partly luck. [A4, conjecture]

144. Become who you are. [verse]


## XIV. God, or Nature

*Read, not shown.*

145. God, read as Nature, has no plans for us. Plans belong to minds in Nature, not to Nature. [D-Nature, reading]

146. On this reading Nature aims at nothing. What looks like aim in it is a work, or a desire, and both are in it. [D-Nature, D-work, reading]

147. It is the nature of reason to regard things as necessary, under a certain form of eternity. [scholium]

148. From the third kind of knowing comes the intellectual love of God, and blessedness is that love. [D-Nature, scholium]

149. Whether anything exists eternally is left open. Something of the mind is eternal, Spinoza said. Undecided. [D-exist, scholium]


## XV. Method

*How sure is any of this?*

150. Human actions can be treated as if they were lines, planes, and bodies. [method]

151. The definitions stand first on the page but were found last, worked back from what the claims needed, and that is said openly. Only the start is first both ways. [method]

152. A declared reading is stated so you can reject it. It adds nothing to any score. [method]

153. If you want your confidence to match the evidence, then when the evidence runs out, say undecided. [method]

154. The easiest whole to fool is yourself. [method]


## XVI. Death

*Every whole ends.*

155. Where death is, I am not; where I am, death is not. [A1, scholium]

156. The start holds while the thinking goes on. A whole ends when its organization comes to a full stop. [A1, D-whole]

157. Death is a whole's term ending. The river ends; the water goes on. [D-whole, verse]

158. A free person thinks of nothing less than death; his wisdom is a meditation on life. [scholium]


## XVII. At the Seam †

*The core has no ethics axiom, and these verses say so.*

159. From striving comes a good: what is useful to our persisting. That is prudence, not an ought, and these verses stop there. [scholium]

160. † A person is where the cells' good and the society's good meet, and ethics begins at that seam. [P1, not derived]

161. † What the good seek for themselves, they want for others too. [not derived]

162. † The true end of the state, Spinoza wrote, is freedom. [not derived]


## XVIII. Close

163. The cell, the body, perhaps the society: the same requirements, in forms that change completely at each scale. The pattern does not change. [PR1, A4]

164. I exist. Everything else I hold as firmly as it holds up. [A1]

165. *Freedom grows as necessity is clearly understood in the first person. In the third person, it is necessity still, and neither outranks the other.* [thesis]

166. All things excellent are as difficult as they are rare. [scholium]

# Part Two: Notes

Spinoza is cited by part and item (E1P15 = Part I, Proposition 15; D definition, A axiom, P proposition, S scholium, C corollary). Lines that echo Spinoza are paraphrases checked for sense against the copies in `sources/spinoza/`, not quotations of a translation. Other sources are the ones the entries cite, or the reports carried over from v11 with their v11 citations. "v11 n" means the verse is kept from v11 verse n.

**House rules.** (1) Only A1 is beyond revision; definitions support nothing alone. (2) "Part" means member (D-whole, Definition 9), except in reports of other authors; any piece of a thing is a *component*. (3) "First person" and "third person" are A2's two descriptions; v12 no longer uses "inside" for the felt side, because A2 says the book does not. (4) "Must" belongs to PR1 (requirement); "necessity" is modal. (5) A declared reading adds no confidence (METHOD rule 5). (6) How whole a thing is never measures how sure we are of it.
