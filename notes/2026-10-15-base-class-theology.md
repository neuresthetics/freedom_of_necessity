# Base class theology (working note, 2026-10-15)

A tool for analysis, not book vocabulary. This note records how we worked out how to fold what the great minds said about God back into the book. The term itself should appear in the book once at most, in the notes.

## The idea

In programming, a base class is the parent every other class inherits from. Read theology that way:

- **The base:** one knowable order. One order exists, it runs by law, and a mind can come to know it.
- **The subclasses:** each tradition keeps the base and adds overrides. God is a person, God made the world from outside it, God answers prayer, God is the world itself. These overrides are what traditions argue about, and they can't be settled from the base alone.
- **The contract:** a legitimate override changes how the base is described but keeps it, as Newton's lawgiver and Einstein's harmony do. A miracle in the strict sense, God breaking the order, is the one move that violates the contract.
- **All the way down:** the knowable order points to Nature, and it extends all the way down to it. The base class is Nature. Every faith is a subclass of Nature, whatever it adds about God.
- **Spinoza** is the limit case. He reached the root and stopped adding: the base class is God, and nothing is left to override.

**In one sentence:** every religion is built on the same foundation, that the world runs by an order we can come to understand, and each faith only adds its own beliefs on top.

**In the fewest words, the base class is:** one knowable order.

## The line the book keeps

"The base is Nature" can be stated plainly and argued for. "Nature is God" stays a declared reading (D-Nature) that each reader declares or doesn't. That way a believer and an atheist can both accept the base, and they only part ways at the override. It's a tool, not a judge.

## Evidence (preliminary)

The Neuresthetics Genius Study sample as of 2026-10-15 has 71 of 441 core people coded, mostly from Christian-era Europe, so it is not a random sample. When they speak of God, they almost always speak of order:

- 23 describe a creator who governs a lawful world rather than stepping in day to day.
- 5 make God the order itself: Spinoza, Einstein, Planck, Schrödinger and Heisenberg.
- Almost none say God works by breaking nature's rules.
- 39 of the 71 can't be scored yet.

This is suggestive evidence, cited from the study, not proof.

## How it enters the book (proposed, not yet drafted)

1. **A scholium on D-Nature**, in plain words. Every way of speaking of God inherits one knowable order found in Nature, and the readings differ only in what they add.
2. **Two or three verses in the Nature chapter**, after verse 53. For example: *When the great minds speak of God, they almost always speak of order. They part over whether it is a person, and over whether it is the world itself. What none of them drops is one order, knowable, found in Nature.*
3. **The study quotes go in the notes**, not the verses.
4. **The freedom chapter** gets the payoff. If freedom is knowing causes, the base is what has to be known.

The core is not rewritten. Nothing here is scored.
