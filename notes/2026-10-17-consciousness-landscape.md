# Consciousness: how the field maps onto the book (working note, 2026-10-17)

A working note, not book text. It compares the book as it stands (core v2 in `entries/`, verses v12) with the main positions in eighteen Grokipedia pages on consciousness, as snapshotted on 2026-10-17. Claims drawn from those pages are cited as *article title* plus sentence id (for example *Hard problem of consciousness* C0001). They are Grokipedia's claims, not checked here. Book citations are entry ids and v12 verse numbers. Nothing in the core or the verses is changed by this note.

Pages read: Integrated information theory; Giulio Tononi; Christof Koch; The Feeling of Life Itself; Global workspace theory; Stanislas Dehaene; Higher-order theories of consciousness; Attention schema theory; Predictive coding; Orchestrated objective reduction; Neural correlates of consciousness; Models of consciousness; the science of consciousness; Consciousness; Hard problem of consciousness; Panpsychism; Artificial consciousness; Anil Seth.

## 1. What the book actually says about consciousness

- **The start leaves feeling out on purpose.** D-I defines thinking by Descartes's list, not by awareness: "It is not defined by being felt" (v10). So A1's certainty is that thinking is going on, not that experience is going on. Whether a machine that says "I exist" is doubting is left open (v11; D-I Scholium 2).
- **Mind is the body's organization, described in the first person.** That is A2's clause (b), a *declared reading*, not scored (v19–21). The evidence (lesions: Broca, H.M.; v16–18) fits it equally well and fits its rival, that the body produces a second thing (v20). The book chooses one thing over two on grounds of economy (v21), after Spinoza (E2P7 S; E2P13; E3P2 S; v22–23).
- **It rejects two positions outright, as part of that reading:** "no ghost beside the body and no illusion laid over it" (v122). That rules out substance dualism and illusionism.
- **The hard problem is left open, and the book says so.** "Why any of it is felt ... stays undecided" (v25, chapter II); "As 25 left it: undecided" (v123, chapter XI); A2, "Limit," citing Chalmers 1995.
- **It claims less than Spinoza.** Spinoza held all things animated in degrees (E2P13 S); the book claims one body, mine (v26). A2 "neither adopts nor rejects Spinoza's wider claim."
- **Wholeness is not tied to feeling.** A3 grades today's AI as falling short of wholeness and adds that this "says nothing about whether they think, or whether anything is felt" (v77; A3). P2's collective mind is steering with signals and memory, and "That it feels is not claimed" (v115; P2, step 6 and Scholium 2). The word "mind" there "doesn't carry the first person across" (v117).
- **Two descriptions, for choices.** "Every choice is one event with two descriptions ... Neither outranks the other" (v136), and the first-person description is often the better causal one (v137). These are *thesis* verses resting on A2's reading and on 135‡ ("every event is caused," not yet an entry).
- **Freedom is knowing causes and acting from that knowledge** (v144, thesis; also v142, v148–149, v173). The core has no freedom entry.
- **Frontier.** "Consciousness is the frontier, and freedom is the question it carries" (v128); the Preface says the same.

What v12 no longer says: v11 had a felt "inside" and "outside" wording, a salience-network verse (v11 78) and a triple-network "candidate correlate" verse (v11 84, after Menon 2011). v12 dropped all three: house rule 3 retires "inside," and no v12 verse or core v2 entry mentions any brain network.

## 3. Sidestepped, or not

**The book does not sidestep the metaphysics.** It takes four clear positions: the start is thinking, not feeling (D-I); mind and body are one thing told two ways, as a declared reading (A2(b)); dualism and illusionism are both out (v122); the hard problem is open and named (v25, v123). On the pages' own map that places it near Type-B identity in logic and dual-aspect monism in form, without the Type-F claim that the ground is experiential.

**It does sidestep the science.** It names no mechanism. Every theory on these pages (workspace, Φ, higher-order, predictive, quantum) answers the question "which organization?", and the book answers only "this body's." v12 removed the one mechanism v11 had offered (the triple network).

**And it holds back a stance it has the tools for.** The works and the self-production test are the very properties biological naturalists and enactivists tie consciousness to. The book grades them carefully and then says, every time, that the grade says nothing about feeling (v77, v115, v126). That restraint is honest, but it is also where the book is closest to the live debate.

## 4. Biggest gaps and tensions

1. **Which organization is the mind?** A2 says "the body's organization." The pages localize to parts of the brain (*Neural correlates of consciousness* C0001; *Global workspace theory* C0004), and Koch's review describes non-conscious routines that keep the body alive (*The Feeling of Life Itself* C0069). The book agrees that most of what the body does never reaches the I (v124). So either "mind" in A2 means only part of the organization, or the first-person description covers what is never felt. A2 needs a line on this.
2. **Does wholeness bear on a first person?** If it does, the cell is a whole too (P1, v103), and the book inherits the combination problem (*Panpsychism* C0211) without IIT's exclusion rule (*Integrated information theory* C0129). If it does not, the book has to say what *does* mark the bodies a first person goes with, beyond "mine." At the moment the only first person the book claims is the author's (v26, v120).
3. **The declared reading has no defence against zombies and Mary.** Economy (v21) favours one thing over two, but the conceivability and knowledge arguments (*Hard problem* C0083, C0151) are arguments that the two descriptions can come apart. A collider pair on A2(b) would want them as the opposing side, with a falsifier stated up front.
4. **Thinking without feeling, feeling without thinking.** D-I strips feeling from the start (v10), while A2's first person includes feeling. The bridge between them (does the A1 thinker feel?) is not stated. This is also exactly where IIT parts from the book, since IIT's first axiom is experience itself (*The Feeling of Life Itself* C0099).
5. **Freedom leans on consciousness without saying how.** v137 claims the first-person description is often the better causal description. That needs the first person to be a real level of organization, and the book has deliberately not said which. Libet-style evidence (*Consciousness* C0105) is not addressed, though the thesis survives it.

## 5. Open questions this raises (for the author)

- Should A2 say which part of the body's organization the first person describes, or say plainly that it leaves this to evidence?
- Should the book state once that grading wholeness is not grading consciousness, as a general rule rather than case by case?
- Should a candidate correlate come back (v11 84 or a successor), tagged ‡, or stay out until there is an entry for it?
- Does A2(b) want a collider pair against the zombie and knowledge arguments?
